# About The **LLM Prefill & Decode Speed Visualizer** is an open-source, interactive tool for reasoning about LLM inference performance. It was built on a simple observation: most people pick hardware for local LLMs by vibes and marketing numbers, when the physics of prefill (compute-bound prompt ingestion) versus decode (bandwidth-bound token generation) tells you almost everything you need to know. ## What it does The tool simulates two distinct phases of LLM inference: - **Prefill** — the parallel pass that ingests your entire prompt at once. Its speed is limited by GPU compute (FLOPS) and it determines your time-to-first-token (TTFT). - **Decode** — the sequential pass that generates one token per step. Its speed is limited by memory bandwidth and it determines your time-per-output-token (TPOT), which is what you feel as "tokens per second" while reading a response. On top of those two primitives it models KV-cache VRAM growth by architecture and precision, prefix caching savings across agentic turns, speculative decoding speedups, continuous-batching throughput decay, documented engine launch-flag deltas (llama.cpp and vLLM), and dollar cost per million tokens including electricity. ## Who it's for Engineers choosing hardware for local inference, developers budgeting cloud or self-hosted deployments, and anyone who wants to understand *why* an LLM feels fast or slow — with real numbers instead of marketing claims. ## Under the hood Every number shown in the UI comes from a deterministic JSON API. The full math is auditable: each endpoint documents its formulas in the [OpenAPI spec](/api/spec) and returns intermediate values (not just final answers) so results can be checked by hand. Benchmark data comes from [LocalMaxxing](https://www.localmaxxing.com) community runs with confidence intervals attached. ## Contact and source Source code and issues: [GitHub — riceharvest/llm-prefill-decode-visualizer](https://github.com/riceharvest/llm-prefill-decode-visualizer). See also the [privacy policy](/privacy.html) (short version: no telemetry, no cookies, no tracking) and [agent guidance](/llms.txt) for programmatic use.
